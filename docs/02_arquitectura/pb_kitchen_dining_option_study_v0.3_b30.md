# PB kitchen and dining option study — b30

**Status:** owner-requested architectural option; recommendation only; not adopted and not for construction  
**Version:** 0.3-option-30-PB  
**Date:** 2026-08-21  
**Scope:** ground-floor kitchen counter/island length, dining position and domestic-bay use  
**Source:** owner request of 2026-08-20; active PB b29; architectural programme v0.6; D-014, D-029, D-071; open D-024 and CF-006

## Architectural conclusion

The owner's visual diagnosis is correct. The current kitchen is undersized relative to
the house and to its own programme, and the problem is amplified by placing kitchen and
dining side by side in only the Side A half of the 10.50 × 18.00 m domestic bay. The
opposite Side B half under the upper floor is materially underused.

The preferred test is therefore a **full domestic-bay kitchen composition on Side A**, a
**7.20 × 1.25 m clear-span island/counter with eight seats**, and the existing 12-seat
dining table relocated to **Side B opposite the kitchen**. The full 4.00 m central
pedestrian axis remains empty.

“Full width” should mean the full usable architectural composition, not a literal
wall-to-wall island. The study retains 1.40 m and 1.90 m clear routes at the two island
ends. Extending the island through those routes would turn it into a barrier between the
hall, pantry and Great Wall and is not recommended.

Its weaknesses are scale, insufficient appliance/landing length, an overly compressed
kitchen–dining composition and poor use of the opposite half of the domestic bay.

## Operational layout

Keep refrigerator, freezer and ovens together in a 2.40 m tall block. Use the remaining
wall length for landing, hob, preparation, sink, dishwasher, waste and a secondary
coffee/storage zone. The final sequence must follow the selected appliance products;
the b30 module labels are a planning test, not a fabrication schedule.

Keep water, drainage and cooking extraction on the Side A wall. The island should remain
dry except for accessible power/data. This avoids new drainage through the industrial
slab, reduces service loops, simplifies extraction, and preserves a clean, durable social
counter.

The dining move has one real trade-off: serving the formal table requires crossing the
wide central axis. This is acceptable only if the eight island seats handle normal daily
meals and the 12-seat table is mainly for family gatherings and events. A robust mobile
serving trolley can support that route. If the owner intends to use the formal table for
every meal, the more efficient fallback is a 4.80 m island with dining retained on Side A.

## Cost and scope reading

The active drawing shows only 4.50 m of wall run plus a 3.60 m island, but cost chapter 20
already carries control allowances of 12 m of kitchen cabinets, a 4.80 m island and 14 m
of countertops within the low-confidence COP 25 million kitchen total.

The b30 option measures approximately 10.05 m of wall composition plus 7.20 m of island:
17.25 m combined versus 16.80 m in the cabinet/island control allowances. With 2.40 m of
tall appliances rather than countertop, exposed worktop length is approximately 14.85 m
versus the 14 m control allowance. The total lengths are therefore close to the cost
model, although the larger island can carry a higher unit cost for structure, seams,
edge finish, drawers, power and installation.

No price, saving, increase, phase-total change or target change is recognized by this
unadopted option. If selected, remeasure cost items 20.01–20.06 and obtain comparable
quotes using national modular carcasses and standard sheet/slab modules before freezing
the design.
