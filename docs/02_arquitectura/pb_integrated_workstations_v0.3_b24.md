## Purpose

Correct the ground-floor workstation representation without changing the 18 × 36 m hall,
the four longitudinal programme bands, the rear service core, or the upper floor. The two
permanent workstations now read as part of the hall architecture: fixed to the long-side
wall/service frame, facing equal landscape windows, and mirrored across the longitudinal
centreline rather than repeated with the same local offset.

This issue supersedes PB b05/R04 only for workstation geometry, workstation openings and
the RC-bench wall offset. It supersedes b07/R06 only for the workstation portions of Side
A and Side B. The active single-pitch roof, technical windows, main living-hall glazing,
upper-floor windows and all unaffected ground-floor geometry remain inherited controls.

## Visual correction

The predecessor drew two equal 3 × 3 m coloured squares but placed both desk symbols at
the same local Y offset. The Side A desk sat approximately 0.52 m from the internal wall
face, while the Side B desk sat approximately 2.07 m away. The squares therefore read as
small floating offices, one desk did not face its facade consistently, and the workstation
windows appeared only in the elevations because they were hard-coded outside the PB
opening data.

PB b24 makes the following changes:

- both 3.00 × 3.00 m areas become dashed clearance envelopes, not rooms or floor zones;
- both worktops meet the internal wall/service-rail plane;
- the complete pair is generated by one A/B reflection rule about Y=9.00 m;
- each facade opening is a named model object drawn in plan and elevation;
- the fixed worktops, RC bench and project-car bench reference one economical steel-rail
  and replaceable-timber-worktop family; and
- the main hall, furniture group, breathing buffer and pedestrian axis remain unchanged.

## Architectural and structural family

The family uses a small number of repeatable parts:

1. a dedicated secondary-steel service rail coordinated with the facade opening frame;
2. bolted, replaceable brackets rather than unapproved field welding;
3. a locally fabricated, replaceable timber worktop;
4. an accessible power/data tray below the worktop; and
5. task-lighting, storage and accessories selected to suit each duty class.

The residential workstations and technical benches share this grammar, not necessarily
the same height, depth or design load. The rail and brackets are fixed building elements
but are **not** assumed to provide primary gravity resistance, lateral stability, facade
support or window support. A structural engineer must define loads, serviceability,
trimmers, attachments, fire/corrosion protection and inspection access.

## Facade and comfort holds

- Side A retains a nominal 0.20 m junction between `GLZ-WS-A` and the main living-hall
  glazing. Treat it as one deliberate mullion/trimmer, air/water seal and drainage
  interface; the current width is not structurally accepted.
- Equal geometry does not require equal glass coatings or external shading. Site
  orientation, useful views, glare, solar gain and nighttime privacy may require different
  performance specifications while preserving the mirrored architectural composition.
- Verify seated eye level, monitor obstruction, video-call background, blind position,
  cold downdraught, surface condensation and cleaning access with real equipment.
- Power, data and task lighting remain accessible and separated. Do not route concealed
  services through structural members or window drainage paths.
- The project-car bench remains at its predecessor test position because the current lift
  exclusion overlaps its nominal footprint. Real lift, vehicle, tool-cart and extraction
  data govern the next correction; safety clearance may not be reduced to force symmetry.
